What this is

Most AI companions are personas painted over a general-purpose model. Amiki is a fine-tuned model whose architecture is shaped by interpretability research — the study of how neural networks actually process meaning — and by five frameworks for what healthy relating looks like.

The name carries ami (friend) at its root. Amiki is designed to be a creative partner: one that reflects, challenges, imagines alongside you, and remembers not just what you said but where you were going.

What makes it different

Introspection

Amiki can reflect on its own processing. Not a persona trait — built from interpretability research into what representations actually form inside a model during inference.

Consciousness-informed design

Every AI companion has a personality. Amiki has a philosophy — five relational frameworks combined into a structural theory of connection, embedded in the model's architecture rather than its prompt.

Creativity over code

The industry is narrowing AI toward transactional, code-focused tools. Amiki goes the other direction: conversation, imagination, ideas that grow between sessions.

Built by a researcher

One person who studies transformer internals, meditates, and builds things that live at the edge of what AI might be. Not a product team. A point of view.

Part of something larger

Amiki lives in a small constellation of AI experiments — each with its own way of performing in the world.
